Architecture · 2026

Bury Your
Loved Ones

Only seeds and soil carried across oceans; rammed earth chunks become graves, to bury is to build.

Studio
2026 Spring Core Options Studio
How to Build a Ruin
Instructor
Stephanie Kyuyoung Lee
Year
2026
Discipline
Architecture

Through investigating diasporas and funerals, I imagine this seed institute as an anchor for immigrant and refugee families. Local residents embed artifacts into the rammed earth walls during construction. We dig up chunks of rammed earth and artifacts, using them as memorials for their loved ones. We return year after year, following our own ritual as the cemetery grows.

Across the path, bio-based polycarbonate encloses a green house. We stash seeds in our bags. Carried across continents, tamarinds, persimmons, peppers, and garlic tell our own family stories.

A century later, when the rammed earth spread in pieces across the hill and the greenhouse overgrown with vegetation, we will be buried.

Installation · 2025

The Unfinished
Swan

A pavilion of useless machines; each one interfaces the game world with the human body.

Program
CMU Summer Undergraduate Research Program, 2025
Collaborators
Leyu Ding, Allan Chen, Ryan Shen
Year
2025
Discipline
Installation

This project explores the architectural properties of a virtual world. Through analyzing the game The Unfinished Swan, we converted the game's interactive features into diagrams. We then converted these diagrams into physical objects that translates the virtual experience to an interactive pavilion of "useless" machines.

Games are defined by systems of input, output, and interfaces. We define our machines as an interface into the game world, in order to find tales of whimsy in the real world.

Through the rules of the virtual world, these machines teach us the meanings of our body.

Data Viz · 2026

Epstein
Flight Logs

Spatiotemporal data reveals a hidden network among elites; color-coded strings and digital maps trace the connective tissue of crime.

Course
62-275: Fundamentals of Computational Design
Spring 2026
Collaborators
Max Whalley, Lukas Yao
Year
2026
Discipline
Data Visualization

We aim to highlight these flight logs as connective tissues to Epstein and his co-conspirators. Through looking into the frequency of flights, the number of co-conspirators onboard, and the historical context, we may reveal the predatory network of Jeffrey Epstein as we visualize our data. We intend to use destinations and departing airports as anchors; using color-coded strings to represent individuals onboard, we want to emphasize the interconnectedness of the co-conspirators and contextualize their travels in the narrative of Epstein's crimes.

Our research question: how does temporal and locational data connect to media narratives to reveal underlying information?

Architecture · 2025

Between
Roots

A building embedded in terrain; children learn everything the soil has to offer before reaching above.

Studio
2025 Fall Poesis Studio II
Environmental Middle School
Instructor
Jongwan Kwon
Collaborator
Estee Teo
Year
2025

This project explores the integration of youth education with environmental design methods. We embedded the building within site terrain to emphasize a connection to the ground. Features such as greenroof, rain garden, and rewilding helio immerse inhabitants within nature.

The school's form evolved from pinching and exaggerating curves, which were derived from an abstract field. We utilized model-making as our main iterative process, expressing curves through carving and bending different materials.

We imagine the students to grow towards the ground and learn everything the soil has to offer, before reaching out above.

Fabrication · 2026

Sticks and
Stones

A grid calibrated to a rock; an accidental perch becomes an intentional resting place.

Course
62-275: Fundamentals of Computational Design
Spring 2026
Collaborators
Max Whalley, William Ivansco
Year
2026
Discipline
Fabrication

This project is an exploration of our ontological understanding of chairs. We interact with the world through intuition: a chair is obviously a chair, while a table is obviously a table. These intuitions create boundaries — we fit ourselves into rigid environments where the definition of an object restricts us to a predetermined experience. Treating a rock as an architectural site, we use digitally fabricated modules as interfaces to break down the boundaries of intuition.

Through research we arrived at a set of definitions for a chair:

  • A chair has to have a backrest on which you could lie and relax your core.
  • A chair has to be above ground level in a room.
  • A chair has to have a seat.
  • A chair has to be intended for one.
  • A chair has to be made to be sat in.

From these definitions, we approached the rock as a site, attempting to turn this completely non-chair object into a chair. Through overlaying a grid on top and manipulating different grid orientations, we are able to intersect the rock at different points, lines, and surfaces. We then established a sitting plane and a backrest plane. Our chair exists between these planes and the rock — at one point, the rock becomes an intentional sitting device.

Urban · 2022

Cities;
Impractical

Impractical artworks chip away at unfamiliarity; until the space belongs to the inhabitants.

Studio
First Year Studio Poesis II
Multigenerational House and Daycare
Instructors
Gloria Chang, Tommy CheeMou Yang
Year
2022
Discipline
Urban / Architecture

My parents grew up in courtyards built by colonizers. But their homes were built by the people. Each window decorated with layers of red leftover from the previous spring festival. This multigenerational community was built upon shared desire to call a place home. That is what I design my multigenerational house to be. I developed the design through collages, models, drawings, and renders.

The house is built for a family of artists, whose impractical and larger-than-life artworks guide them towards the idea of "home". Through taking up space, art chips away unfamiliarity.

As a daycare, the family teaches children how to make impractical art, to claim space. Together, they paint along the vast exposed walls, install giant sculptures, and turn this space into "home".

Cities (houses), often built for efficiency and profit, thus become impractical.
